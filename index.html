Research focus

Multicellular models of bone remodeling

My research combines tissue engineering, advanced microscopy, biological experimentation, and quantitative analysis to examine how bone tissues form, resorb, and regenerate.

01

Functional bone models

Three dimensional multicellular systems that reproduce sequential bone formation, resorption, and reformation in vitro.

02

PTH regulated remodeling

Investigation of how intermittent and continuous PTH exposure patterns influence osteoblast, osteoclast, and matrix behavior.

03

Quantitative imaging

Longitudinal two photon microscopy, SHG imaging, Imaris reconstruction, and MATLAB based analysis of tissue remodeling.

Research approach

Following bone remodeling through time

Rather than relying only on endpoint measurements, I use longitudinal imaging to track cellular and extracellular matrix changes throughout multiple remodeling phases.

This approach enables direct comparison of bone formation, resorption, and reformation within the same three dimensional tissue environment.

Learn more about the research
01

Formation

Osteogenic cells generate mineralized bone like tissue and collagen rich extracellular matrix.

02

Resorption

Osteoclast lineage cells remodel the tissue and generate localized resorption regions.

03

Reformation

Osteoblast lineage cells respond to the remodeled microenvironment and regenerate new matrix.
