Who I am

From clinical questions to living systems

I am an Egyptian dentist and tissue-engineering researcher interested in how cells communicate, reorganize, and collectively shape tissue behavior over time.

My clinical background introduced me to the challenges of craniofacial tissue damage and regeneration. Research gave me another way to approach those problems: instead of looking only at the final tissue outcome, I became interested in the cellular interactions that produce it.

At The University of Tokyo, my doctoral research has focused on functional three-dimensional bone models and the spatiotemporal coupling between bone-forming and bone-resorbing cells. I combine experimental biology with longitudinal imaging, molecular analysis, and quantitative computation to study remodeling as a dynamic process.

I am particularly interested in understanding not only what changes in a biological system, but when, where, and how interactions between cells create that change.

How I work

Experimental, visual, and quantitative

My work developed across several disciplines rather than around a single technique.

Tissue engineering

Three-dimensional bone models, organoid and spheroid culture, primary-cell workflows, osteogenic differentiation, and osteoblast-osteoclast co-culture.

Advanced imaging

Two-photon microscopy, fluorescence imaging, second harmonic generation imaging, tissue clearing, and three-dimensional reconstruction.

Molecular biology

Real-time PCR, immunofluorescence, DNA recombination, molecular labeling, and lentiviral and retroviral transduction.

Quantitative analysis

MATLAB-based image and data analysis, longitudinal measurements, statistical workflows, data visualization, and computational interpretation of biological datasets.

Scientific communication

Experimental design, manuscript development, peer-review response, scientific presentations, figures, and visual communication of complex biological processes.

Building beyond the laboratory

Coding, web development, and visual design allow me to approach scientific problems and communication from perspectives beyond conventional laboratory training.

Beyond the laboratory

Curiosity rarely stays inside one field

Science is the center of my work, but not the limit of my curiosity.

I am interested in architecture, natural structures, coding, visual design, languages, and the ways complex systems organize themselves.

These interests influence how I think about biology. Architecture makes me consider structure and function. Coding makes me think in systems and relationships. Design makes me think about clarity. Direction

Understanding biology well enough to influence it

My long-term direction is to develop and use controllable biological models that reveal how cells coordinate tissue-level behavior.

I am particularly interested in questions spanning regeneration, remodeling, aging, cellular communication, disease modeling, therapeutic screening, and three-dimensional tissue organization.

The specific biological system may change. The underlying question remains similar: how do individual cellular behaviors become coordinated tissue-level outcomes, and how can that process be measured and eventually controlled?
