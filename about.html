Engineering better models of bone regeneration.

I am a PhD candidate at the University of Tokyo working at the intersection of tissue engineering, bone biology, advanced imaging, and quantitative analysis. I develop three dimensional functional bone models and use longitudinal four dimensional imaging to study how cells communicate, remodel their environment, and determine regenerative outcomes over time.

Beyond individual techniques or tissues, I am fascinated by how living systems organize, interact, adapt, break down, and rebuild. This website is also an expression of that curiosity through science, movement, structure, coding, and design.

Research focus

Understanding how multicellular systems remodel and regenerate

My current research uses functional bone models as a dynamic experimental system to investigate how cellular communication, spatial organization, signaling, and time shape tissue level remodeling and regenerative responses.

01

Functional bone models

Three dimensional multicellular systems that reproduce sequential bone formation, resorption, and reformation in vitro, allowing remodeling to be studied as a continuous biological process.

02

PTH regulated remodeling

Investigation of how intermittent and continuous PTH exposure patterns alter osteoblast, osteoclast, osteocyte, and matrix behavior throughout remodeling.

03

Quantitative imaging

Longitudinal two photon microscopy, SHG imaging, three dimensional reconstruction, and computational analysis to follow cellular and matrix changes through space and time.

Research approach

Following remodeling through space and time

Rather than relying only on endpoint measurements, I follow the same living tissue environment longitudinally to understand how cellular behavior and extracellular matrix organization evolve throughout remodeling.

This approach allows formation, resorption, and reformation to be examined as connected phases of a dynamic multicellular process, revealing relationships that may be missed when tissues are analyzed only at isolated endpoints.

Learn more about the research
01

Formation

Osteoblast lineage cells organize, communicate, and generate mineralized bone like tissue within a collagen rich extracellular matrix.

02

Resorption

Osteoclast lineage cells interact with the formed tissue, reshape the matrix, and generate localized resorption regions.

03

Reformation

Osteoblast lineage cells respond to the remodeled microenvironment and regenerate new matrix, completing the transition from resorption back to formation.

Integrating biological, imaging, and computational approaches

My work combines experimental biology with quantitative and visual approaches to understand complex multicellular behavior across different spatial and temporal scales.
