Research overview

Using bone remodeling to study dynamic multicellular behavior

My doctoral research uses functional bone models as controllable experimental systems for understanding how cellular interactions, signaling, spatial organization, and time determine tissue-level remodeling and regenerative outcomes.

01
Doctoral research

Functional bone remodeling models

I develop and maintain multicellular three-dimensional bone models that reproduce sequential tissue formation, resorption, and reformation. These systems allow osteoblast and osteoclast behavior to be studied within a self-generated bone-like extracellular matrix while preserving the temporal sequence of remodeling.

Long-term 3D culture Bone remodeling Multicellular systems Matrix regeneration
02
Pharmacologic regulation

PTH exposure and remodeling dynamics

I investigated how the frequency and duration of PTH(1-34) exposure regulate bone remodeling across formation, resorption, and reformation. The work examines matrix loss and recovery, cellular distribution, spatial coupling, and molecular responses associated with remodeling over time.

PTH signaling Formation Resorption Reformation
03
Imaging and analytics

Longitudinal imaging and quantitative analysis

I combine two-photon microscopy, second harmonic generation imaging, fluorescent cell tracking, tissue clearing, Imaris reconstruction, and MATLAB-based analysis to follow cellular and extracellular matrix changes longitudinally. This converts three-dimensional tissue models into four-dimensional datasets that reveal how remodeling evolves through time.

Two-photon imaging SHG Imaris MATLAB ImageJ and Fiji
Experimental workflow

Following remodeling through multiple phases

Longitudinal imaging allows the same three-dimensional tissue environment to be followed across formation, resorption, and regenerative recovery rather than being evaluated only at isolated endpoints.

01

Formation

Osteoblast-lineage cells organize and generate mineralized bone-like tissue within a collagen-rich extracellular matrix.

02

Resorption

Osteoclast-lineage cells interact with the formed tissue, remodel the matrix, and generate localized resorption regions.

03

Reformation

Osteoblast-lineage cells respond to the remodeled microenvironment and generate new matrix, linking resorption back to tissue formation.

Methods

Integrating experimental biology, imaging, and computation

My research combines biological experimentation with advanced visualization and quantitative analysis to examine multicellular behavior across different spatial and temporal scales.

Tissue engineering

Functional bone models, spheroid and organoid culture, osteoblast and osteoclast co-culture, long-term culture, multicellular labeling, and remodeling assays.

Advanced imaging

Two-photon microscopy, SHG imaging, fluorescence tracking, tissue clearing, micro-CT, and three-dimensional reconstruction.

Quantitative analysis

Imaris segmentation, MATLAB-based workflows, ImageJ and Fiji, longitudinal measurements, statistical analysis, and scientific visualization.

Future direction

From observing living systems to learning how to direct them

My broader goal is to develop experimental systems that allow us not only to observe how cells and tissues behave, but eventually to understand, manipulate, and guide their collective behavior.

Questions I want to pursue

  • How can communication between cells be visualized and quantified as tissues change through time?
  • Can genetic, signaling, or pharmacologic interventions steer multicellular behavior toward desired regenerative outcomes?
  • How do spatial organization and time determine whether tissues regenerate, deteriorate, or develop age-associated dysfunction?
  • Can engineered multicellular systems become controllable platforms for drug screening and regenerative intervention?
  • How can advanced imaging, computation, and biological engineering reveal processes that remain hidden in conventional endpoint experiments?
