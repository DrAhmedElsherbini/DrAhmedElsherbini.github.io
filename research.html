Research

Three dimensional models for regenerative bone medicine

I develop experimental and analytical platforms that reveal how cells, signaling pathways, and extracellular matrix dynamics shape bone remodeling and regeneration.

Research overview

My central objective is to build controllable tissue engineering systems that connect mechanistic bone biology with translational regenerative strategies.

Doctoral research

Functional bone remodeling models

I develop and maintain multicellular three dimensional bone models that reproduce sequential formation, resorption, and reformation. These systems support controlled study of osteoblast and osteoclast coupling within a self generated bone like matrix.

Pharmacologic regulation

PTH exposure and remodeling dynamics

I investigate how intermittent and continuous PTH(1 to 34) exposure patterns regulate tissue level remodeling. The work evaluates matrix loss and recovery, cellular distribution, spatial coupling, migration, and molecular readouts relevant to bone formation and resorption.

Methods platform

Longitudinal imaging and quantitative analysis

I use two photon microscopy, second harmonic generation imaging, fluorescence based cell tracking, CUBIC and LUCID tissue clearing, Imaris three dimensional reconstruction, and MATLAB based analytical pipelines to follow matrix and cellular changes over time.

Future direction

Controllable platforms for therapeutic regeneration

My long term research direction integrates biomaterials, three dimensional bone models, cellular signaling, quantitative imaging, and regenerative therapy development.

Areas of interest

  • Biomaterial scaffold design and cell instructive microenvironments
  • Stem cell differentiation and immunomodulatory bone repair
  • Osteocyte signaling, sclerostin, Wnt pathways, and tissue coupling
  • Craniofacial, alveolar, and musculoskeletal bone regeneration
  • Reproducible imaging and open quantitative analysis workflows
